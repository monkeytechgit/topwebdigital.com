How much does a small business website cost in San Diego in 2026?

Real 2026 pricing for small business websites in San Diego: what each range includes, hidden costs to avoid and how to choose the right option.

“How much does a website cost?” is like asking how much a car costs: it depends on what you need. Here are real 2026 market ranges for San Diego and what to expect at each level.

Price ranges in 2026

  • $0–$500: DIY builders and generic templates. Fine to start, but hard to rank and limited.
  • $600–$2,000: a custom professional site for a local business with basic SEO, forms and click-to-call. The sweet spot for most small businesses.
  • $2,000–$6,000: online stores, booking systems, bilingual sites or integrations.
  • $6,000+: portals, custom software or enterprise projects.

What a professional site should include

  1. A domain in your name (not the agency's).
  2. Fast hosting and SSL.
  3. Mobile-first design.
  4. Contact form, click-to-call and text buttons.
  5. Basic SEO: titles, descriptions and speed.
  6. Search Console and Analytics setup.
  7. Training to edit it yourself.

Hidden costs to avoid

  • Renewal pricing: what do hosting and domain cost from year two?
  • Domain owned by the agency: if you leave, you lose your address.
  • Paid edits: some agencies bill for every text change.
  • Expired theme licenses: the site stops updating and becomes insecure.
A cheap website nobody finds costs more than an investment that brings in customers every month.

How to know if it's worth it

Do the math: if your average sale is $150 and your site brings just 5 new customers a month, that's $750 monthly. A $1,290 site pays for itself in under two months. Try our lost-revenue calculator.

How to choose who to hire

  • Ask for case studies with results, not just pretty designs.
  • Get delivery dates in writing.
  • Confirm the domain will be in your name.
  • Ask what happens after launch.
